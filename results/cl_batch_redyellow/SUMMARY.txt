That matches the "red treated as yellow" row on pass/fail, but NOT its prediction:
that row says the red-word runs in B should look at the yellow person, and they
looked at the red one (59.7 % vs 36.6 %). No row of the table fits. The table did
not anticipate the pattern that the data show:

  The unseen colour draws the robot regardless of the instruction. In B the red
  person is in view ~59 % whether the word is "red" or "yellow", and the trained
  word "yellow" loses to it. In A the instruction still moves attention somewhat,
  in both directions, which is what put the paired score just over the line.

Reading
- No evidence that the policy grounds the unseen word "red". The red-word runs
  looked "right" in both scenes only because the robot goes to the red person
  anyway.
- The purple/yellow discrimination it did learn is brittle: a distractor in a colour
  never seen in training weakens the trained word (purple, scene A) or overrides it
  (yellow, scene B).
- So the earlier results hold -- within purple and yellow the policy follows the named
  colour, and the colour swap showed that is the cue it uses -- but they are
  in-distribution. "Understands colour words" is not supported.

Why red might be salient is not established here. Plausible: red is out of
distribution for the crops, and the model's action head was never trained to ignore
it; or red is simply more saturated/contrasting against the grey room. A test with
an unseen but low-salience colour would separate those; not run.
