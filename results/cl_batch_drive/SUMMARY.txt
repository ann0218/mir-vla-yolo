Reading
- With its forward speed published, the policy follows the named colour clearly and
  consistently: 11/11 pairs on both scores, the most consistent of all batches.
- It keeps the named person at 2.8 m on average, farther than the expert's 2.0 m, and
  has them in view about half the time -- no better than when it could only turn.
  The earlier guess that forcing v = 0 had understated in-view time is not supported.
- It is not safe on its own: 9 of 23 runs needed the ground-truth emergency stop, mostly
  because it drove close to the OTHER person, and one run tipped from a sudden jump in
  forward speed. Before any demo: add a forward-acceleration limit and a sensor-based
  stop that does not depend on ground truth.

==== CORRECTION 2026-09-15 (after this batch) ====
1. The "front-lidar wall limit" copied from follow_expert.py watches the REAR arc.
   /scan is virtual_laser_link, yaw 0 against base_link, angle_min -180 deg, so beam 0
   points straight behind (measured: +180 deg = 7.98 m back wall, +-90 deg = 6.00 m side
   walls). follow_expert.front_clear() treats beams around index 0 as ahead. The expert
   collected its data with this, and this batch reproduced it: the wall limit here
   reacted to what was behind the robot, not in front.
2. The lidar does not see the people at all (no collision geometry): purple at
   5.35 m / -4 deg read 8.00 m. A lidar-based stop could never have prevented the
   close approaches above; only the camera sees the people.
3. The v jump that tipped s11_yellow (+0.52 m/s in a tick) is not unusual for the
   expert either: 10.7 % of its forward speed-ups exceed 0.3 m/s per tick.
The next batch (cl_batch_drive_safe) uses the true forward arc, a camera-based stop,
a forward-acceleration limit, and logs the ground-truth distance without acting on it.
