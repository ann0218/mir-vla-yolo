Against the pre-registered criterion: borderline pass (p = 0.039 / 0.052), far weaker
than the trained pair (purple/yellow: 11/12, p = 0.002) and carried entirely by the
red-word runs; the purple word lost its selectivity.

Two readings fit this scene equally well, and scene B (yellow + red) separates them:
  (1) the policy grounds "red", but red and purple look alike to it, so "purple"
      becomes ambiguous next to a red person;
  (2) the policy mostly learned a yellow detector -- "yellow" = the yellow person,
      "purple" = the non-yellow one. With no yellow present "purple" has nothing to
      go on, and "red", nearer to yellow in hue, is handled like "yellow".
Under (2) "red" in scene B should send the robot to the YELLOW person.

==== Update after scene B (2026-09-15) ====
Scene B (yellow + red) showed no instruction effect (paired p = 0.84 / 0.58), and the
red person was in view ~59 % whether the word was "red" or "yellow". Read together, the
borderline pass here is not evidence that "red" is grounded: the robot is drawn to the
unseen colour regardless of the words, and in this scene the instruction still shifted
attention somewhat. Neither reading (1) nor (2) above fits scene B. Full joint reading,
including the per-scene instruction effect, in ../cl_batch_redyellow/SUMMARY.txt.
