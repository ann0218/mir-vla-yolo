Reading
- The box dimensions learned. Offline the policy localises the named person almost exactly
  (11 px median, 87:1 against the distractor), and it does so from the instruction: the two
  people are identical apart from colour.
- Localisation degrades once the policy drives itself: 38 px median, 67 % within 60 px. That
  is distribution shift, the reading DESIGN.txt anticipated -- the frames it now sees are
  ones the expert never produced.
- The failure mode is NOT confusing the two people. Of 9241 steps where it claims a box and
  the named person is in view, only 483 (5 %) land on the other person; 3684 (40 %) land on
  neither -- vague localisation rather than mistaken identity.
- Control is unchanged within noise, so the interpretability is close to free at this scale.
- Caveat: offline "box on the named person" is measured against labels that were themselves
  produced by YOLO plus ground-truth matching, so it shows the model reproduces that
  labelling, not that the labelling is right. The eyeball check on six frames is the only
  independent evidence that the labels are correct.
- Caveat: a sharper offline test -- both people in view, the DISTRACTOR nearer the image
  centre -- gave only 23 scorable frames (named 13, other 3, neither 7, median error 49 px).
  Too few to conclude, but it hints the model is pulled towards whoever is central.

Next, if this is taken further
  draw the predicted box on a recorded closed-loop clip, which is what makes the diagnosis
  legible to a reader; and test whether "neither" steps coincide with the turns that lose
  the person.
